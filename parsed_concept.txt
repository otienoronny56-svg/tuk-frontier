EXECUTIVE SUMMARY
TUK Frontier Hackathon is Kenya's premier student-led engineering and technology innovation event, jointly convened by the Office of the Faculty Representative, the KUZA–TUK Chapter and ASA–TUK, within the Faculty of Engineering and the Built Environment (FEBE) at the Technical University of Kenya.
The hackathon convenes engineering, technology and built-environment students alongside industry leaders, government agencies, development partners and innovation ecosystems to co-create technology-driven solutions to Kenya's most pressing challenges.
Aligned with Kenya Vision 2030, the Bottom-Up Economic Transformation Agenda (BETA), the Sustainable Development Goals and the African Union Agenda 2063, the hackathon transforms student potential into market-ready solutions, startups and employment-ready graduates.

BACKGROUND AND RATIONALE
Africa's median age of 19 gives it the largest youth demographic dividend in history, and Kenya — anchored by Nairobi's Silicon Savannah — has proven that homegrown innovation can compete globally in fintech, agritech, healthtech and geospatial technology. Yet a persistent gap remains between university theory and the applied, technology-driven competencies the labour market demands. Graduates routinely enter the workforce without hands-on exposure to collaborative problem-solving, rapid prototyping, entrepreneurship, or tools such as cloud computing, AI, GIS and IoT.
Hackathons at leading universities — MIT, Stanford, Harvard — have proven that intensive, challenge-driven formats compress semesters of learning into days of doing, connecting students to real industry problems and catalyzing startup formation. TUK Frontier Hackathon is FEBE's answer to this imperative: a purpose-built platform that channels TUK's student talent directly into Kenya's development priorities.
Kenya's tech sector is growing at an estimated 8.5% annually, with Nairobi ranked among Africa's top five startup cities and supported by initiatives such as Konza Technopolis and the Kenya National Innovation Agency (KeNIA). Yet the university-to-startup talent pipeline remains under-structured. TUK Frontier Hackathon addresses this gap as FEBE's flagship, annually recurring innovation platform.
ABOUT THE ORGANIZING INSTITUTIONS
The Technical University of Kenya
TUK is a public university established under the Universities Act, 2012, and a successor institution to the Kenya Polytechnic University College, with a history spanning more than five decades. Located in Nairobi's Central Business District, TUK is anchored on practical, applied and technical education, producing graduates who are industry-ready from day one.
Faculty of Engineering and the Built Environment (FEBE)
FEBE is one of TUK's flagship faculties, comprising Civil and Structural Engineering, Electrical and Electronics Engineering, Mechanical Engineering, Geospatial Engineering, Geoinformatics, Architecture, and Building and Construction Technology. It serves as TUK's primary engine for innovation in infrastructure, spatial planning and emerging technology convergence.
Office of the Faculty Representative — FEBE
The constitutionally recognized student leadership structure within FEBE, bridging the student body, Faculty administration, industry partners and the broader TUK community. As co-convener, it provides institutional authority, mobilizes the student community, coordinates cross-departmental logistics, and safeguards the hackathon's student-led character.
KUZA–TUK Chapter
KUZA is a pan-African, student-led movement harnessing geospatial technology, earth observation, GIS, remote sensing and spatial data science for sustainable development. As co-convener, the KUZA–TUK Chapter anchors the hackathon's technical and geospatial dimension — contributing domain expertise, industry networks, challenge design and mentorship coordination.

• Spatial Data Science & Digital Mapping      • AI & Machine Learning for Geospatial Applications

ASA–TUK (Architecture Students Association of The Technical University of Kenya)
ASA–TUK is the representative student body for TUK's Department of Architecture, championing design excellence, sustainable building practices and creative approaches to the built environment. As co-convener, ASA–TUK anchors the hackathon's architectural and urban-design dimension — contributing design-thinking methodology, sustainable infrastructure expertise and creative direction to challenge tracks addressing smart buildings, urban planning and climate-responsive design.

VISION, MISSION AND STRATEGIC DIRECTION
Why 'Frontier'?
A frontier is the edge of the known — the threshold of possibility where the curious push beyond convention. In naming this hackathon 'Frontier', the organizers signal a deliberate ambition: for TUK students not merely to learn about emerging technologies, but to stand at their edge — building, experimenting, iterating and solving problems that matter. Ours is a frontier defined by artificial intelligence, geospatial intelligence, climate technology, smart cities and space technology.
Vision
To be East Africa's leading student-driven innovation platform, producing technology solutions, startup enterprises and employment-ready graduates that power Kenya's and Africa's digital transformation.
Mission

CHALLENGE TRACKS
TUK Frontier Hackathon organizes teams around six strategic challenge tracks, each aligned with Kenya's national development priorities. Interdisciplinary teams are strongly encouraged.
TRACK
FOCUS
1. Geospatial & Earth Observation Innovation
GIS, remote sensing, satellite imagery, drone data and spatial analytics for urban planning, disaster risk, environmental monitoring and climate adaptation. Anchored by KUZA–TUK.
2. Artificial Intelligence & Data Science
AI applications, ML models, NLP and data analytics for health, education, agriculture, finance and public service delivery.
3. Smart Infrastructure & Built Environment
Smart buildings, structural health monitoring, BIM-enabled project management and sustainable energy integration. Anchored by ASA–TUK.
4. Climate Tech & Environmental Engineering
Clean energy, water management, carbon monitoring, green infrastructure and sustainable agriculture solutions.
5. Health Technology & Bioinformatics
Medical device prototyping, telemedicine, health data analytics, disease surveillance and AI-assisted diagnostics.
6. Financial Technology & Digital Inclusion
Fintech for financial inclusion, digital payments, alternative credit scoring and SME finance.

Monitoring, Evaluation & Sustainability
A structured M&E framework tracks participant satisfaction, solution quality, startup formation, employment outcomes, media reach and partner satisfaction, with post-event impact reports shared with all stakeholders. Long-term sustainability rests on diversified funding, alumni engagement, institutional FEBE support, and annual growth in the partner base — growing the hackathon from a faculty event into TUK's flagship annual innovation festival.
